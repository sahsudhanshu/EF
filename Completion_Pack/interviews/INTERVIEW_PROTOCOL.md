# Interview protocol (Group 31)

**Purpose.** Add primary qualitative evidence to RQ5 (fraud-related grievances, security friction, support) and test two assumptions in the venture model (RQ6): that institutions would pay for a payment-safety service, and that end users would use it.

**Target: 8 to 10 short interviews (15 to 25 minutes each).** More is better, but 8 gives a defensible small qualitative sample for a term paper. Aim for:

| Group | n | Who | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Small merchants | 3 | Shop owners or street vendors who accept UPI/QR | Receive payments; see failed payments, fake screenshots, support problems |
| B. Newer or less confident users | 4 | Include at least 1 woman, 1 person aged 55+, 1 person who recently started using UPI | Closest to the Findex gaps (lock use, payment use) |
| C. Institution staff | 1 to 3 | Bank branch staff, fintech or payment-provider employee, business correspondent | Tests whether an institution would buy or pilot a safety service |

Recruit through family, neighbours, local shops and classmates' contacts. Do not interview anyone who is a close friend of the interviewer if it can be avoided, and do not offer payment.

## Ethics (read before starting)
- Check with the TA/instructor whether the course needs formal ethics approval for interviews. Say in the paper what was decided.
- **Never ask for or record**: UPI PINs, OTPs, passwords, account or card numbers, phone numbers, Aadhaar, or names of people who defrauded them.
- Use verbal consent (script below). Each participant gets an ID (M1, U1, I1...). Keep names and contact details only in a separate private list, never in the repo.
- If someone describes a fraud they suffered, stop if they get upset. Mention the national helpline 1930 and their bank's complaint channel. Do not give financial or legal advice.
- Audio recording only with explicit consent; otherwise take notes. Delete audio after writing notes.
- Do not interview anyone under 18.

## Consent script (read aloud)
"We are students doing a course research project on how people use mobile payments and what makes them feel safe. It takes about 20 minutes. Taking part is voluntary, you can skip any question or stop at any time. We will not ask for any PIN, OTP, account number or personal identifiers. We will write notes (or record audio only if you agree) and use only anonymous quotes with a code like U3, not your name. Is it OK to go ahead? May I take notes? May I record audio?" -> Record the answers on the notes template.

## Interview guide (semi-structured; adapt wording and language)
**Warm-up (all)**
1. How do you use your phone for money (UPI apps, bank app, cash)? Since when?
2. Which apps do you use, and which do you trust most? Why?

**Safety and exposure (A, B)**
3. Have you ever received a call, message or request for money that felt suspicious? What happened? (Do not ask for details that identify anyone.)
4. What do you do when something looks suspicious? Who do you ask?
5. Do you use a screen lock or app PIN? What makes it easy or annoying? (Probe: older phones, shared phones.)
6. Have you ever had a payment fail or money get stuck? What did you do? How long did it take to resolve? Was support helpful?

**Support and trust (A, B)**
7. If something went wrong with a payment today, how would you try to get help?
8. What would make you trust a payment app more?

**Concept test (all)** Show the one-paragraph description: "A short audio and text guide in your language, sent through your bank or payment app, that explains common scams, how to check a request, and how to reach official support. It never asks for your PIN or OTP."
9. Would you use something like this? When would it help? What would put you off?
10. In which language and format (audio, video, text, WhatsApp)? Who else in your family would need it?

**Institution staff (C only; replaces 3 to 8)**
11. What are the most common fraud or payment complaints you see from customers?
12. How are customers educated about scams today? How do you know if it works?
13. Would your institution consider a service like this? Who would decide? What budget range would be realistic per month for a pilot? What would you need to see first (security review, evidence, integration effort)?
14. What are the biggest obstacles (procurement, regulatory, data, staff time)?

**Close (all):** Anything we did not ask that you think matters? Thank you; give the helpline 1930 and complaint-channel information if relevant.
